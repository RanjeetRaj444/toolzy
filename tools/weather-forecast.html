    How Weather Forecasting Works

    Modern weather forecasting relies on numerical weather prediction models operated by meteorological organizations across the globe. These models process real-time data gathered from weather satellites, weather balloons (radiosondes), radar systems, and ocean buoys to compute future atmospheric states using thermodynamic physics equations.

    Understanding Common Weather Metrics

    • Temperature (°C / °F): The thermal energy in the air measured in the shade.
    • Relative Humidity (%): The percentage of moisture the air holds relative to the maximum moisture it could hold at that temperature. High humidity combined with high heat makes outdoor conditions feel significantly hotter.
    • Wind Speed & Direction: Air movement caused by uneven atmospheric pressure gradients. Wind direction indicates where the wind originates from (e.g. Northerly winds blow from north to south).
    • Feels Like: The apparent temperature, factoring in humidity and wind chill — often different from the raw air temperature.
    • UV Index: A 0-11+ scale for sunburn risk. 3+ calls for sunscreen; 8+ is very high exposure.
    • Sunrise & Sunset: Local sunrise and sunset times for the selected location's own timezone.
