How Electricity Bills Are Calculated

Your electricity consumption is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh), commonly referred to on utility bills as units. One unit is equal to 1,000 watts of electrical power consumed continuously for one hour. Your monthly electric bill is calculated by multiplying total units consumed by your local tariff rate, plus any fixed service charges and taxes.

Appliance Consumption Formula

To find how much power an individual appliance uses:

Daily kWh = (Appliance Wattage × Hours Used Per Day) ÷ 1,000

Daily Cost = Daily kWh × Rate per Unit

Average Wattage of Common Household Appliances

  • Air Conditioner (1.5 Ton): 1,500 – 2,000 watts
  • Refrigerator: 150 – 250 watts (runs intermittently)
  • Ceiling Fan: 60 – 75 watts
  • LED Television (55"): 80 – 120 watts
  • Microwave Oven: 800 – 1,200 watts
  • LED Light Bulb: 9 – 12 watts

Frequently asked questions

What is 1 unit of electricity?

1 unit of electricity equals 1 kilowatt-hour (kWh), which is 1,000 watts of electricity used over one hour. For example, running a 1,000-watt iron for one hour consumes exactly 1 unit.

How can I reduce my monthly electricity bill?

Switching to 5-star inverter appliances, using energy-efficient LED lighting, setting air conditioning to 24°C–26°C, and unplugging idle chargers can reduce power consumption by 20% to 35%.

Why does my actual bill differ from this estimate?

Electric utilities frequently use tiered slab billing (where higher units cost more per unit), fuel adjustment surcharges, and municipal taxes which vary by region.
