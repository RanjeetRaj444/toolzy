Generate Random UUID v4 Identifiers in Bulk

A Universally Unique Identifier (UUID), also known as a Globally Unique Identifier (GUID), is a 128-bit value standardized by RFC 4122. Version 4 UUIDs rely on cryptographic pseudorandom numbers to guarantee uniqueness across distributed databases without a central coordinating authority.

Structure of a Version 4 UUID

A UUID v4 is represented as 32 hexadecimal characters grouped into five sections: xxxxxxxx-xxxx-4xxx-yxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx. The '4' designates the version, and 'y' (8, 9, a, or b) designates the variant.

Frequently asked questions

Could two generated UUIDs ever collide?

With 122 bits of true randomness, the probability of generating a duplicate UUID is approximately 1 in 2.71 quintillion—essentially zero in real-world computing.

Are these UUIDs cryptographically secure?

Yes. Toolzy utilizes the browser's native crypto.getRandomValues() API for hardware-backed entropy.

Can I generate multiple UUIDs at once?

Yes. You can generate batches of UUIDs in bulk and copy them formatted for database seeding or API testing.
